Abstract

From Web evidence
to trustworthy defense.

Threat reports published on the Web, such as vendor blogs, security advisories, and incident write-ups, are where defenders first learn how an attack unfolds. Large language models (LLMs) can now read these reports at scale, but what they produce feeds directly into defense decisions. A fluent answer is not enough: every step from report to action has to be trustworthy.

This tutorial follows threat knowledge from reports to defense and beyond, organized around four properties: traceable knowledge, faithful reasoning, reproducible scenarios, and verifiable outcomes. For each stage, we survey representative methods, benchmarks, and the gaps that remain open, and show how its output becomes the input of the next stage. The tutorial closes with an integrated demonstration and a discussion of open questions.

Tutorial overview

Starts with Web threat reports

Original cyber threat intelligence (CTI) reports

  1. 1 Evidence

    Traceable

    Structured attack representation

    Can extracted attack knowledge be traced back to its sources?

    Passes on

    Attack representations with source-linked evidence

  2. 2 Hypotheses

    Faithful

    Reasoning over threat knowledge

    Do LLM judgments stay faithful to what the threat reports support?

    Passes on

    Reasoning conclusions, uncertainty, and hypotheses to be tested

  3. 3 Scenarios

    Reproducible

    Attack scenario specifications

    Can the environment an attack needs be specified precisely enough to re-enact the attack?

    Passes on

    Environment requirements and attack scenario specifications

  4. 4

    Verifiable

    Attack–defense co-evolution

    When attack and defense agents evolve against each other, can each confrontation’s outcome be verified rather than self-reported?

    Delivers

    Range-based execution, outcome verification, and iterative improvement

Target audience

Researchers, graduate students, and practitioners in Web information retrieval, natural language processing, and cybersecurity who want to use LLMs to turn Web threat reports into actionable security knowledge.

Prerequisites

Basic familiarity with LLMs. Essential security concepts will be introduced during the tutorial.
